Case study · The sales enablement project · Feb to Sep 2026

From 13th of 15 reps to #1 of 17, while a 10-hour sales day shrank to 4 to 8 hours.

Brooks Golden built an AI system around a CRM his seat had no API access to. It answered leads in minutes, drew the customer's product before they asked, filled in the quotes, reminded him to call, kept the pipeline clean and handed every sale to production. This page walks through each piece, inside the CRM, with the numbers behind it.

Built and run by Brooks Golden, founder of Keelwater, while working as a product specialist on a made-to-order sales team (branded inflatables).

The baseline

February: 13th of 15, and the day ran 10 hours

The job: every inquiry needs a fast, specific reply, a picture of what the customer would be buying, a quote, follow-up until they answer, a phone call, and a clean handoff once they buy. In February, every one of those steps was typed by hand in the CRM, 1 deal at a time, across roughly 100 new leads a month.

The rep seat had no API access. So the system could not be a simple integration. It had to use the CRM the way a person does: through the screen.

The result

Close rate more than doubled

From 10.5% in February to 23.0% in April, the first full month with the system, when he went from 13th to 1st. He stayed at or near the top through the summer, while replying faster, sending 4 times as many emails, and working fewer hours.

Close rate is the company's own measure: orders from new customers divided by new leads.

01
Respond

The first reply, in minutes, about their project

Speed wins custom work. The system read each inquiry the moment it landed and wrote a reply about the customer's actual project, not a template.

Before

Every inquiry waited for Brooks to open it, read the form, look at the reference photo and write back. Overnight and weekend leads waited longest. Median wait in February: 24 hours.

What the system does

A sweep checks for new leads every minute from 7:30 am to 9 pm on weekdays. It reads the form, the photo and the deadline, drafts a specific reply, releases overnight leads at 8 am, and reloads the deal to confirm the email really went.

Result

Median wait fell to 35 minutes by July, and 60% of July's inquiries got a reply inside an hour. Customers answered 59% of first replies, up from 37%.

“Frequently they would say, wow, thank you for getting back to me so quickly. It was so good they didn't even know it was AI.”Brooks Golden
02
Show

A picture of what they're buying, before they ask

“How are you going to buy an inflatable if you don't know what it looks like?” Every quote email carried 3 concepts of the customer's own product, drawn from their form and reference photo.

Before

Describe it in words, or wait days for a designer's mock-up. Deals stalled while the customer tried to picture it.

What the system does

Generates 3 concepts per quote with image models, embeds them in the quote email, and links real photos of similar past projects from a 31-category photo catalog.

Result

The conversation moves from what it might look like to which one they want. Customers pick a concept by number and ask for the price, as in both deals on this page.

03
Quote

Quotes without the line-by-line

The CRM had an Auto Quote panel: pick a format and it drops a blank quote template into the deal's history. Then someone had to fill in every line. The system fills it.

Before

After each call, 10 to 15 minutes per quote (Brooks's estimate): remember the call, type product, size, material, prices, add-ons and discount into the template, line by line.

What the system does

Starts from the call transcript, the form and the pricing rules. Picks standard or options, fills the same template, attaches the reference image, and publishes a quote page with its own link.

Result

Quotes can go out the same day as the call, priced by the same rules every time. The automation alone published 328 quote pages from June 3 to September 18.

04
Quote

Every quote in 1 searchable database

Every quote page lands in 1 index: date, rep, customer, type, line items, materials and deal number. When a customer asks for the same thing as a past order, the answer is a search away.

662 quotes, June 3 to September 18328 written by the automation269 by a second rep using a packaged copy of the tool

The tool spread: it was packaged for a second salesperson, who produced 269 quote pages with it between June and September. When another rep adopts a tool, that is the clearest sign it was worth building.

05
Respond

Open the deal, and the reply is already written

When a customer wrote back, the system read the whole deal, every email, call and quote, and saved a draft reply in the CRM for Brooks to check and send.

Before

Reread the thread, remember what was said on the call, find the quote, then write. With dozens of conversations open at once, replies slipped.

What the system does

Drafts the reply inside the deal with the full history in view, and knows which concept they picked or what they complained about. Brooks reviews before anything sends.

Result

Over 1,000 reply drafts from May to September. From late July, every customer email was also tracked against a 20-hour answer deadline.

06
Follow up

Nothing slips through the cracks

Deals die in the middle. Every stage ran on a follow-up cadence, and watchdogs checked every deal for a next step and every customer email for an answer.

Before

Follow-up from memory. A quiet deal surfaced weeks later, if at all.

What the system does

Stage-based follow-ups at set intervals, sent on weekdays in the hours people reply, stopped the moment the customer answers. A watchdog flags any open deal with no next step. Quiet deals get a genuinely new idea, not a bump.

Result

1,509 follow-ups scheduled from April to September: 460 sent automatically and 875 drafted for Brooks to approve.

07
Relationship

The reminder to pick up the phone

“What builds relationships? Actually getting on the phone with the customer.” Email closes sales; calls build relationships. So the system made sure the call happened.

Before

Calls got crowded out by admin. The easy path was 1 more email.

What the system does

24 hours after a new lead, if the customer had not replied, it writes a note on the deal: call or text them, with the text already written, and labels the deal so it stands out.

Result

78 call reminders from July to September. 92 more were skipped because the customer had already replied.

“There are a number of deals I closed just through email. You don't really build a relationship; you're just making a sale. Having automations that remind the account executive to get on the phone is powerful.”Brooks Golden

10
Hand off

The handoff to customer service and production

When a deal closed, the people building it needed everything: what was sold, the size, the material, the deadline, and every decision made along the way. The system wrote it into the Asana card for them.

Before

Rewrite the deal from memory for the production card, then again for the customer's handoff email.

What the system does

Keeps 1 Project Vision & Summary note on every deal, the Asana card, updated from the emails, calls, quote and chosen concept. On payment, a handoff email goes out with a shipping estimate.

Result

Production starts from the customer's actual decisions, like “use AI design #3”. 266 handoff emails drafted.

Timing

Jobs that run at specific times

Timing is a feature. New leads are checked every minute during business hours, overnight replies wait for 8 am, follow-up drafts are written at night so they are ready in the morning, and the repair crew works on a fixed cadence so it doesn't burn through AI usage.

Reliability

Who fixes it when the screen changes

Working through a screen is fragile: buttons move, a draft can look saved and not be, 2 jobs can reach for the same browser. The answer was a small AI crew with separate jobs, and 1 rule above all: a task is only done when the result is confirmed on a fresh load of the deal.

The job checks its own workEvery email, note, activity and card is confirmed on a fresh reload of the deal before the job calls itself done.
QA bot: diagnose and proposeReads the ticket and the evidence, finds the cause, proposes the smallest fix.
Architect: approve or reviseReviews the fix, and fixes the whole class of problem when the same failure keeps coming back.
Auditor: trust nothingChecks the deployed fix against the live deal. “Tests passing is not proof. The promise being kept is proof.”

What this means for a tax or CAS firm

Different product, same pattern

A firm's week has the same shape: inquiries to answer, proposals to price, documents to chase, questions to answer from a long history, and handoffs from the person who sold the work to the people who do it. This is what that looks like with the same approach.

1 First reply in minutes

New client inquiries answered the same hour with a reply about their situation, and the discovery call booked.

2 A picture of what they buy

A clear preview of the deliverable: the engagement scope, the monthly close package, the checklist they will get.

3 Quotes without line-by-line

Engagement letters and proposals drafted from the call transcript and your pricing rules, ready to review.

4 Every quote in 1 place

Every proposal searchable, so pricing stays consistent across partners and seasons.

5 Replies already written

Client emails answered in drafts from the full client history, reviewed by staff before sending.

6 Nothing slips

Document requests, e-signatures and organizers chased on a cadence, with a watchdog on every deadline.

7 The call

A prompt to call the client at the moments that build trust, not just another email.

10 The handoff

Sales to onboarding to production, with every decision already written into your workflow tool.

And the part that matters most for firms: it does not depend on your software having an API. If a person can do it on the screen, the system can do it on the screen, safely, on a schedule, and prove it did.
